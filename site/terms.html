IHoldings and status

A holding is a territory, enclave or island group with one representative point (its capital or main port) and a sequence of control periods. A country such as India is several holdings: British India, the princely states shown, and the French, Portuguese, Dutch and Danish enclaves.

Scope is every holding of Britain, France, Portugal and the Netherlands between 1800 and 2025, and any other power while it held one of them (for example Japan in the Dutch East Indies, 1942–45). "Colony" means any control stronger than soft power: colonies, protectorates, League mandates and UN trusts, chartered-company rule, condominiums, leases and military occupations. Informal empire is excluded.

The map shows the state at 1 July of the selected year. Periods are end-exclusive and must join without gaps or overlaps; the build stops if they do not.

Independence year
The first sovereign period after colonial rule, with stated exceptions in independence_overrides.csv (India and Pakistan 1947, Bangladesh 1971, Sri Lanka 1948, Vietnam 1954, and the Statute of Westminster dates for dominions).
Cross-check
Dates are compared with COLDAT (Becker 2019). Differences are definitional and listed in docs/GAZETTEER_NOTES.md: COLDAT ends dominions at 1919–20, ignores wartime occupations, and treats West New Guinea as part of Indonesia.
Confidence
Each period is rated high or medium. Medium means the date is debated or simplified; the record card shows “date debated”.

Which flows
Pairs where at least one side is a holding, or former holding, that exists in that year. The globe shows the 50 largest of these for the empires selected; clicking a holding shows its 20 largest partners.
Line width
Ranked within the year among the flows shown, in six classes from the percentile rank. The largest shown flow is always widest. Widths compare flows within a year, never across years.
Ratio
A→B exports against B→A exports, rounded to the nearest simple fraction with terms from 1 to 5 (beyond 5 : 1 it reads “> 5 : 1”). If only one direction is recorded, the flow is marked “one-sided estimate”.
Aggregates
Where sources report a group (for example “British West Indies”), it is placed at a representative holding and named as an aggregate. The crosswalk is data/curated/trade_codes.csv.
Metropole share
Trade with the holding's colonial power (or, after independence, its last one) divided by the holding's total recorded bilateral trade. The erosion curve takes the median across former holdings by years since independence, where at least three holdings have data.

IIIDevelopment systems

Each economy is described by dimensions that can be compared with three reference models: the country's own former coloniser (as it was c.1945–65), the Soviet model, and the US / Bretton Woods model.

Hand-coded for the 21 curated countries, by decade: planning regime, planning institution and monetary arrangement; and once: legal origin and colonial administration. Automatic for all ex-colonies: UN General Assembly voting agreement with each reference country, and, as context only, trade openness and government share of GDP.

The inheritance index is the mean similarity to the former coloniser across the available dimensions. The nearest model is whichever of the three references has the highest mean similarity in that decade. A dimension persists until its similarity to the coloniser first falls below 0.75.

What it shows
Resemblance, not cause. A high inheritance index says a system looked like its coloniser's; it does not say why.
Openness
Kept out of the similarity score: large economies such as the US are closed, so comparing openness levels with them made every closed economy look American.

IVEstimates and uncertainty

Values
All money values are approximate, in current prices of the source's unit. No deflation or currency conversion is applied between periods.
Sparse years
Colonial trade records before about 1820 are thin. The site shows what exists and says so; it does not interpolate.
Density strip
Under the time slider, bars count holdings under imperial rule and ticks count transfers in each year, so thin years are visible before you read them.
Review status
Every hand coding starts “unreviewed”. The count of reviewed and flagged rows is shown on Inherited Systems.

VIVersions and updates

Historical layers
Frozen and versioned in the repository; each page shows the date its data were built.
Recent years
A scheduled job will refresh post-independence indicators and the newest trade year when sources publish them (Phase 7). Until then, the “updated” stamps show the last build.
Corrections
Submitted through Correspondence, reviewed, and applied to the curated files; each change is a commit in the repository history.

VIIKnown limitations

  1. Large colonies are single points; internal frontiers and gradual conquest are not drawn.
  2. Only Hyderabad and Mysore stand for the ~560 princely states.
  3. Empires beyond the four appear only while holding one of their territories.
  4. Trade shares depend on what the sources recorded; aggregates and one-sided reports remain.
  5. Hand codings for 21 countries are drafts awaiting review; 73 other ex-colonies are compared on legal origin and UN voting only.
  6. Reference models are stylised profiles of c.1945–65 and do not change over time.
